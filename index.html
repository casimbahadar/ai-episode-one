Make your first clip

Cut 1 of 11About 20 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have three short anime-style clips, and you will know which look you like best.

What you need

  • A Google account. Flow is for adults 18 and over, and it works in Canada.
  • Flow, Google's video tool, at flow.google.com. You do not need to pay. Every account gets 50 credits a day.

How credits work

A credit is what Flow charges for each video. With the Omni Flash 1.2 model, a rough draft at 360p costs 1 credit for each second. A sharper 720p clip costs 3 credits for each second.

So a 5 second draft costs 5 credits, and 50 credits is enough for ten drafts a day. Credits you do not use are gone the next day.

Checked on Google's Flow help pages on 11 Nov 2026. Prices change, so read the cost Flow shows before you generate.

Busy hours

Free accounts sometimes cannot make video at busy times, roughly 2 to 5 p.m. UTC. In Ontario that is about 9 a.m. to noon in winter and 10 a.m. to 1 p.m. in summer. Images still work then.

Steps

  1. Open Flow and start a new project.
  2. In the prompt box, choose the model name, then choose Video.
  3. Set the model to Omni Flash 1.2, the quality to 360p, the shape to tall (9:16), the length to 5 seconds and the number of outputs to 1. Flow charges for each output.
  4. Type your prompt and choose Generate.

Flow's button names may differ a little from these. The steps follow Google's help page as read on 11 Nov 2026.

Your first prompt

Google's advice is to name five things: the subject, the action, the place, the lighting and the style. Here is one to copy.

Hand-drawn 2D anime style with clean line art and flat cel shading. A girl with short silver hair and a yellow raincoat waits at a bus stop at night and looks up as snow starts to fall. Soft light from a street lamp. The camera slowly pushes in.

Describe the look in your own words. Do not name a show, a studio or an artist. That keeps your work original, and it is the rule this whole course follows.

Do this now

Make three 5 second drafts of one idea. Change one thing each time: the style words, the lighting or the camera move. That costs 15 credits.

Download the one you like best, and write down the words that made it work.

You are ready for Cut 2 when you can say which style words gave you the look you want.

From idea to script

Cut 2 of 11About 30 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have a script for a 30 to 45 second episode, written one shot at a time.

Keep the story small

One character wants one thing. Something is in the way. By the end, one thing has changed.

Example: a girl waits for the last bus. It never comes. She walks home and sees that the snow has made her street beautiful.

Think in shots

A shot is one unbroken piece of video. On a free account, Omni Flash 1.2 makes shots of 3 to 10 seconds.

Plan 6 to 9 shots of 4 to 6 seconds each. Short shots cost less, and a mistake is quicker to redo.

Shot lengths checked on Google's Flow help pages on 11 Nov 2026.

Write what we see and what we hear

For each shot, write two things: what we see, and what we hear. Keep speech to one short line per shot. Cut 7 covers talking characters.

  • Shot 1. We see the girl alone at the bus stop at night. We hear wind.
  • Shot 2. We see her check her phone, then the empty road. She says: “It’s not coming.”
  • Shot 3. We see the first snowflake land on her sleeve. We hear soft music start.

Do this now

Open the Shot list tool and enter your shots. The tool adds up the running time and the credits. Aim for 30 to 45 seconds in total.

Open the Shot list

You are ready for Cut 3 when your shot list tells the whole story without you explaining it.

Design a character you own

Cut 3 of 11About 40 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have a character bible and a model sheet: one image that shows your character from the front, the side and the back. Later cuts reuse it so the character looks the same in every shot.

Original means original

Build the character from your own description. Do not start from an existing character and change details. If a friend can name who it reminds them of, redesign it.

Film studios are suing an AI image company over pictures of their characters. No ruling was found when checked on 11 Nov 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

Write the bible first

A character bible is a short page that fixes how the character looks. Open the Character bible tool and fill in the shape, hair, eyes, outfit, colours and what never changes. The tool turns it into one description you can paste into any prompt.

Open the Character bible

Make the model sheet

In Flow, images do not use credits. A free account can make up to 20 images a day with the Nano Banana Pro model and up to 400 a day with the lighter models.

Image limits checked on Google's Flow help pages on 11 Nov 2026.

  1. In your Flow project, choose the model name, then choose Image.
  2. Paste your character description, then add the lines below.
  3. Make one character per request. Asking for several at once tends to blend them.
  4. Pick the best sheet. Then ask for close-ups of the face: calm, happy, sad and surprised.
  5. Save the result as a character in Flow. You can then call it in a prompt by typing @ and its name.

Character model sheet on a plain white background. Full body, front view, side view and back view. Neutral pose. Hand-drawn 2D anime style, clean line art, flat colours.

What you own

In the United States, work made only by an AI tool cannot be copyrighted, because the law requires a human author. Canada has no court ruling yet. The parts you write and arrange yourself, like the bible and the script, are the human-made parts.

Checked on 11 Nov 2026. Sources are on the Sources page. This is general information, not legal advice.

Do this now

Make a model sheet and a face sheet for your main character. Then do the look-alike check in the Character bible tool.

Storyboard and shot list

Cut 4 of 11About 25 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have a rough drawing for every shot and a finished shot list.

What a storyboard is

A storyboard is a row of rough pictures, one for each shot, that shows what the camera sees. Stick figures on paper are fine. Nobody else needs to see it.

Three choices for every shot

  • Shot size. A wide shot shows the place. A medium shot shows what a person is doing. A close-up shows what they feel.
  • Camera. Keep it still unless you have a reason to move it. Use one move per shot at most.
  • What is new. Each shot should show one thing the last shot did not.

Try not to put two shots of the same size next to each other. A change of size keeps the eye interested.

Plan for a tall screen

Draw tall boxes, because phones show video at 9:16. Keep the important part in the middle of the box. Apps place buttons and captions over the bottom and the right edge.

Plan your talking shots now

If a character speaks, make that shot a medium shot or a close-up with the face toward the camera. Cut 7 explains why.

Do this now

Draw one box for each shot. Then open the Shot list and add the shot size and camera move to each entry. Check that the running time is still 30 to 45 seconds.

Open the Shot list

You are ready for Cut 5 when someone else can follow the story from your drawings alone.

A still frame for every shot

Cut 5 of 11About 45 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have one approved picture for each shot. Each picture becomes the start of a video in Cut 6.

Why start from a picture

A video that starts from a picture you approved is easier to control than one made from words alone. Images also cost no credits in Flow, so this is the cheapest place to fix mistakes.

Steps

  1. In your Flow project, choose the model name, then choose Image. Set the shape to tall (9:16).
  2. Type @ and your character's name. You can also add the model sheet as a reference image. Flow calls reference images ingredients.
  3. Open the Prompt builder, fill in the shot, leave the spoken line empty, and paste the prompt into Flow.
  4. Make two to four versions and keep the one closest to your model sheet.
  5. Note which picture belongs to which shot number.
Open the Prompt builder

Check each frame

  • The hair, outfit and colours match the model sheet, and the detail that never changes is there.
  • Hands and faces look right.
  • Nothing in the frame looks like a known character, a logo or broken lettering.

Fix it instead of starting over

Flow can change an image from a short instruction, such as “Make the raincoat yellow”. It keeps the earlier versions, so you lose nothing by trying.

Image editing, references and free image limits checked on Google's Flow help pages on 11 Nov 2026.

Do this now

Make one approved frame for each shot in your list.

You are ready for Cut 6 when your frames, in order, tell the story like a comic.

Turn frames into video

Cut 6 of 11About 60 minutesCost: free credits

By the end of this cut your first three shots will be moving.

Two ways to use your pictures

Frames to Video

Your picture becomes the first frame of the clip. With Omni Flash 1.2 this works on the Flow website, not in the Flow phone app. This course could not confirm how well the website works in a phone's browser. The Veo 3.1 Lite model also does it, in clips of 4, 6 or 8 seconds at 720p for 10 credits each.

Ingredients to Video

You give Flow your character and other reference pictures and describe the shot. With Omni Flash 1.2 this makes clips of 3 to 10 seconds on a free account. Talking shots in Cut 7 use this way, because saved voices only work with ingredients.

Checked on Google's Flow help pages on 11 Nov 2026.

Write a motion prompt

The picture already carries the look. The prompt only needs to say what moves: first the character, then the camera. Use one action and one camera move.

She looks up and holds out her hand as snow starts to fall. The camera slowly pushes in.

Draft cheap, then finish

Test the motion at 360p, which costs 1 credit a second. When a draft works, make that shot again at 720p, which costs 3 credits a second. Set the number of outputs to 1.

Judge each try

  • The character still matches the model sheet at the end of the clip.
  • The movement is the one you asked for.
  • Hands, faces and backgrounds do not bend, melt or flicker.

Most shots need more than one try. Keep the best one and move on.

Busy hours

Free accounts sometimes cannot make video at about 9 a.m. to noon Ontario time in winter, and 10 a.m. to 1 p.m. in summer.

Do this now

Animate your first three shots as 360p drafts, with up to three tries each.

You are ready for Cut 7 when you have one try you would keep for each of the three shots.

Talking characters and lip sync

Cut 7 of 11About 45 minutesCost: free credits

By the end of this cut one character will say a line in their own voice, with the mouth moving to match.

How it works

Omni Flash makes the voice and the mouth movement together, from the line you write in the prompt. Flow will not add or change speech in a video you upload. So a talking shot has to be made with its line in it.

Give each character one voice

  1. Choose the Omni Flash model, then Video, then Ingredients.
  2. Under the prompt, choose Add, then Voices. Each voice has a short sample you can play.
  3. To make your own, choose Create New Voice. Pick a base voice, name it, and describe how it should change, such as “quiet and a little shy”.
  4. In the prompt, type @Voice and the name of the voice.

Voice steps follow Google's Flow help page as read on 11 Nov 2026. Button names may differ a little.

Write the line

Put the words in quotation marks and say who speaks. Use one short line per shot.

Mira looks at the empty road and says: "It's not coming." The camera stays still.

Frame it so the mouth can be seen

Use a medium shot or a close-up, with the face toward the camera and one speaker in the shot. Keep the head fairly still.

Check it twice

  • Watch with the sound off. The mouth should start and stop with the line.
  • Watch with the sound on. The voice should be the right one and every word should be correct.

When it will not match

After three tries, change the shot instead. Show the listener's face while the line is heard. Show the speaker from behind or from far away. Or make the line a thought, spoken over a silent shot. Hand-drawn animation has long used the same tricks.

No independent test of lip-sync accuracy on anime-style characters was found on 11 Nov 2026, so trust your own eyes. Kling and Seedance also make speech with lip sync. Cut 11 shows how to compare them.

Do this now

Save a voice for your main character and make one talking shot with it.

You are ready for Cut 8 when the shot passes the sound-off check.

Voices, music and sound

Cut 8 of 11About 30 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have chosen music you are allowed to use, and your clips will have the sounds you want.

Three layers of sound

  • Voices. You made these in Cut 7.
  • Sound effects. Add a sentence to the prompt that says what we hear, such as “We hear wind and a bus far away”.
  • Music. Add one track in the edit, in Cut 9.

Music that starts and stops inside each clip sounds choppy once the clips are joined. Ask for no music in your video prompts, and add one track over the whole episode later.

Music you are allowed to use

YouTube Audio Library

Free music and sound effects for your YouTube videos, found in YouTube Studio. You can filter for tracks that ask for a credit.

Read on YouTube's help page on 11 Nov 2026.

TikTok and Instagram have sound libraries inside the app. Their rules for each kind of account were not checked for this course, so read them in the app.

AI music

Edit, caption and go vertical

Cut 9 of 11About 60 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have one finished video file.

Choose an editor

Flow has a Scenebuilder for putting clips in order. Phone editors such as CapCut and Instagram's Edits add music, captions and trimming.

The free limits of CapCut and Edits were not checked on the makers' own pages.

Steps in any editor

  1. Start a tall project: 9:16, 1080 by 1920.
  2. Add your best try of each shot, in order.
  3. Trim the start and end of each clip. Cut while something is moving, and remove any frozen moment.
  4. Add your music and lower it until every voice is easy to hear.
  5. Add captions for every spoken line. Read each one and fix the names.
  6. Export at 1080p.

The first two seconds

Open on your most interesting picture. Do not open on a title card.

Leave the AI marks alone

Do not crop out a watermark or strip the hidden AI information from the file. Cut 10 explains why honest labels protect you.

Do this now

Edit the whole episode. Watch it on your phone at arm's length, once with sound and once muted.

You are ready for Cut 10 when the story is clear both ways.

Publish with honest AI labels

Cut 10 of 11About 20 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut your episode will be posted and labelled correctly.

What each platform asks for

  • YouTube requires its AI use setting when content looks real, and when AI music is the main focus of the video. Clearly animated content does not need it.
  • Instagram requires its AI label for realistic AI voices or music, even when the picture is a cartoon.
  • TikTok requires its AI label for realistic content and encourages it for anything made fully with AI.

Read on each platform's help pages on 11 Nov 2026. These rules change every few months.

This course labels every video

Turn the label on each time, even when it is optional. Flow's files carry an invisible watermark and hidden information that say they were made with AI, and platforms can read these. A video that hides it and gets found out loses trust.

Say it in your caption as well, in plain words.

Made with AI tools. Story, characters and editing by me.

Expect mixed reactions

Some viewers dislike AI animation and will say so. Answer politely or not at all. Never claim the work is hand-drawn.

About money

This course does not promise views or income. YouTube does not pay for videos that look mass-produced or repeat one template, so make each episode its own story.

To earn from ads, a YouTube channel needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. YouTube says its Partner Program changes on 1 Feb 2027.

Read on YouTube's help page on 11 Nov 2026.

Reported by other sites, not found on YouTube's page: from 1 Feb 2027 new applicants will need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views.

Plan a series and compare tools

Cut 11 of 11About 45 minutesCost: free

By the end of this cut you will have a one-page plan for a series, and a fair way to decide whether a paid tool is worth it.

Write a series bible

A series bible is one page that keeps every episode consistent. It holds:

  • The idea of the series in one sentence.
  • The cast, with a character bible for each.
  • The style line you use in every prompt, word for word.
  • The places that come back, each with a reference picture of the empty set.
  • One sentence for each of the next five episodes.

Reuse what you built

Model sheets, saved voices, set pictures and your style line carry over to every episode. The story should not. Each episode needs its own beginning, problem and change.

Budget an episode

A 40 second episode with three 360p tries per shot costs 120 credits. Making the kept shots again at 720p costs another 120. That is 240 credits, or about five days of free credits. The Shot list does this sum for your own episode.

Based on Flow's prices as read on 11 Nov 2026.

Compare tools fairly

  1. Pick three shots: one with action, one talking shot and one with two characters.
  2. Pay for one month of one other tool. The Upgrades page lists them.
  3. Make the same three shots on both tools, from the same pictures and prompts.
  4. Judge each on four things: does the character match, is the motion right, does the mouth match the voice, and what did each kept shot cost.
  5. Keep the tool that won, and cancel the other before it renews.
See the Upgrades page
